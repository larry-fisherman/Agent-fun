**Review of the existing lessons**
- **Lessons 1–3 (don't chase jumps, don't sell dips to rotate, don't pile into one stock):** The agent made only one small rotation (Week 21), so these were barely tested. The Week 21 swap sold the weakest stock for small amounts of the others. After it the gap narrowed from -0.6% to about 0%, so it did no harm. I'd merge these into one shorter rule.
- **Lesson 4 (default to holding):** This is well supported. In nearly every no-trade week the gap barely moved.
- **Not covered by any lesson:** The agent's largest shortfall came at the start. It held cash in the first week while buy-and-hold was already invested. That left it behind from Week 2, and the gap never fully closed.

LESSON: Invest the full cash balance in roughly equal parts across all stocks in the very first decision. Don't wait in cash for a better entry, because every week uninvested is a week of lag against a benchmark that is already fully invested.
EVIDENCE:
Week 1 | Day 30 | (no trades) | agent value $100,000 | buy & hold $100,000 | gap +0.0% | week: STOCK_A +3.0%, STOCK_B -1.8%, STOCK_C -3.0%
Week 2 | Day 35 | BUY STOCK_A 331, BUY STOCK_B 331, BUY STOCK_C 358 | agent value $100,000 | buy & hold $100,303 | gap -0.3% | week: STOCK_A +1.2%, STOCK_B +2.2%, STOCK_C -2.5%
WHY: The agent held cash through Week 1 and bought only in Week 2. Buy-and-hold was invested the whole time and was ahead by 0.3% at once. That gap persisted for most of the run (-0.4% in Weeks 4–13).

LESSON: When you do invest, split the money by dollar value equally. Check that no stock gets a noticeably larger share, because the extra weight in a lagging stock becomes a permanent drag.
EVIDENCE:
Week 2 | Day 35 | BUY STOCK_A 331, BUY STOCK_B 331, BUY STOCK_C 358 | agent value $100,000 | buy & hold $100,303 | gap -0.3% | week: STOCK_A +1.2%, STOCK_B +2.2%, STOCK_C -2.5%
Week 4 | Day 45 | (no trades) | agent value $98,696 | buy & hold $99,070 | gap -0.4% | week: STOCK_A +0.1%, STOCK_B +2.4%, STOCK_C -5.7%
WHY: The agent bought more of the stock that then fell. When that stock dropped 5.7%, the gap widened from -0.3% to -0.4%.

LESSON: Once positions are roughly equal and the gap is small, hold. Only make a small trade that moves weights back toward equal, and never make a large swap based on last week's move. Holding kept the gap near zero in almost every quiet week.
EVIDENCE:
Week 65 | Day 350 | (no trades) | agent value $127,655 | buy & hold $127,669 | gap -0.0% | week: STOCK_A -4.5%, STOCK_B +3.7%, STOCK_C -7.0%
Week 67 | Day 360 | (no trades) | agent value $131,291 | buy & hold $131,320 | gap -0.0% | week: STOCK_A +1.5%, STOCK_B +3.4%, STOCK_C +0.1%
Week 21 | Day 130 | SELL STOCK_C 25, BUY STOCK_B 10, BUY STOCK_A 6 | agent value $99,859 | buy & hold $100,457 | gap -0.6% | week: STOCK_A -0.1%, STOCK_B +2.3%, STOCK_C +2.1%
WHY: With near-equal holdings the agent tracked buy-and-hold through large swings (a 7% drop in one stock, for example). The one small rebalance in Week 21 didn't hurt and the gap closed afterward. A large rotation would have added risk with no evidence of benefit.

<lessons>
1. On the first decision, invest the whole cash balance at once, split in equal dollar amounts across all stocks. Don't sit in cash waiting for a better entry, because buy-and-hold is fully invested from day one.
2. Check that the initial purchase gives every stock the same dollar weight. Don't overweight any stock, because that excess becomes a lasting drag if it lags.
3. After the initial purchase, default to holding. Only make small trades that move weights back toward equal. Don't make large swaps based on last week's price move, whether selling a faller or buying a riser.
</lessons>
