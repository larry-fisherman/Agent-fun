**Main patterns**

1. Going to cash, or near cash, and then buying back late. This happened at the sell-off in week 42 and again in weeks 90–94.
2. Constant small trims and re-buys. The agent sold stocks that then rose, and bought after rises.
3. Total exposure fell in the late uptrend. Weeks 59–64 and 90–94 are examples.

<lessons>
1. Do not move the whole portfolio to cash, or close to it, after one down week. Keep most capital invested (roughly 70–80% or more) unless several consecutive weeks of broad decline have occurred. Sold-out positions repeatedly missed the rebound and turned a lead into a deficit.
2. Do not sell a stock in small pieces over several weeks, or sell winners just because they rose, while stocks are trending up. Trim only when a position is above the cap or has fallen for several weeks in a row.
3. Hold positions through ordinary weekly swings of up to about 5%. Do not sell after one drop, and do not buy after one jump. Buying a jump or selling a dip was repeatedly followed by a reversal.
4. Keep the single-position cap at about 50%. Drawdowns still happened at 40–50% concentration, so when one stock is near the cap, trim it to about 35–40% rather than letting it drift up.
5. When well ahead of buy-and-hold, protect the lead by cutting a stock that has fallen several weeks in a row. After cutting, put the proceeds into the other stocks rather than leaving them in cash.
</lessons>
