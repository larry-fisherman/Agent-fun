**Review of the existing lessons**
- **Lesson 1 (default to holding): keep, revised.** The agent mostly held. The gap grew slowly with no trades and widened in steps around the trade weeks. No trade ever closed it.
- **Lesson 2 (don't reverse a switch): drop as a separate rule.** The agent never reversed a switch, so the record neither supports nor contradicts it. I folded the useful part into the new lessons.

LESSON: Don't sell part of a position just because it fell for a week or two. Trim only if the reason has lasted several weeks and you can state it. Partial sales after drops left the agent with less exposure when prices rebounded.
EVIDENCE:
Week 39 | Day 220 | (no trades) | agent value $106,813 | buy & hold $109,385 | gap -2.4% | largest position 47%
Week 40 | Day 225 | SELL STOCK_A 150 | agent value $103,199 | buy & hold $106,335 | gap -2.9% | largest position 45%
Week 41 | Day 230 | (no trades) | agent value $107,331 | buy & hold $111,110 | gap -3.4% | largest position 46%
Week 43 | Day 240 | SELL STOCK_A 150 | agent value $102,555 | buy & hold $105,406 | gap -2.7% | largest position 46%
Week 45 | Day 250 | (no trades) | agent value $99,800 | buy & hold $101,984 | gap -2.1% | largest position 45%
WHY: The sell at week 40 came after a drop. The gap kept widening to -3.4% in the rebound that followed. The week 43 sale did avoid part of a drop (gap -0.5% the next week). That benefit was gone by week 45 (-2.1%). Net, the trims cost return and did not reduce the maximum drawdown meaningfully (-22% vs -23%).

LESSON: Don't move capital into one stock to push it toward half the portfolio. Rotations into a single name raised concentration from about 41% to about 50% without narrowing the gap. Cap any single position near its starting weight unless there is a strong, lasting reason.
EVIDENCE:
Week 32 | Day 185 | (no trades) | agent value $121,102 | buy & hold $123,481 | gap -1.9% | largest position 42%
Week 33 | Day 190 | SELL STOCK_B 100, BUY STOCK_A 82 | agent value $124,501 | buy & hold $126,796 | gap -1.8% | largest position 50%
Week 55 | Day 300 | BUY STOCK_A 119 | agent value $109,633 | buy & hold $116,964 | gap -6.3% | largest position 47%
Week 56 | Day 305 | (no trades) | agent value $114,397 | buy & hold $122,711 | gap -6.8% | largest position 47%
WHY: The week 33 rotation took the largest position to 50% and the gap did not improve. The agent's biggest drop (week 34, about -7.5%) came while it held that 50% position. The week 55 purchase was followed by the gap widening from -4.9% to -6.8%. Concentration added risk and no return.

LESSON: When the agent is already behind buy-and-hold, a trade should not be the response. Each trade week was followed by a flat or wider gap, and the large widenings (weeks 49–55) came while holdings had drifted away from the starting mix. Prefer returning toward the starting equal-weight mix over making new directional bets.

Week 82 | Day 435 | (no trades) | agent value $127,740 | buy & hold $136,102 | gap -6.1% | largest position 41%
WHY: After the earlier sales left the portfolio underweight in the winners, the gap went from -2.1% to -4.9% in two weeks. The week 81 swap did not close it (-5.9% to -6.1%). Selling winners and buying other stocks never recovered the lost ground.

<lessons>
1. Default to holding the starting mix. Change positions only for a clear reason that has lasted several weeks, not because of one week's price move. Every swap and trim in the last run left the agent further behind buy-and-hold.
2. Don't sell part of a position after it drops. Partial sales after declines left the agent underexposed during the rebounds that followed, and they did not reduce the maximum drawdown.
3. Don't rotate capital into one stock to push it toward half the portfolio. Keep every position near its starting weight unless there is a strong, lasting reason. Concentrating to about 50% added risk, and the agent's largest single-week drop came while it was that concentrated.
</lessons>
