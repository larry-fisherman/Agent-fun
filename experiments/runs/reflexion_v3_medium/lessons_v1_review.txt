<lessons>
1. Keep the largest position at about 60–70% of the portfolio at most. Never let one stock reach roughly 90–100%, and trim a winner that grows too large instead of adding to it. The largest drawdowns happened while the portfolio was concentrated in one stock.
2. Avoid high-turnover rotation. Don't sell a stock and buy it back within a few weeks, and don't switch every week into the latest mover. Make a switch only when there is a clear reason, and otherwise hold.
3. Don't sell a large block of a stock right after a sharp one-week drop to chase last week's winner. Rebounds were often missed. Trim gradually and keep the core holding unless the trend has clearly changed.
