**Review of existing lessons**

- Lesson 1 (cap concentration): Partly helped. The agent's drawdown (-13.6%) ended slightly better than buy-and-hold's (-14.5%). Concentration reached 67–69% in weeks 10–14, and the agent still beat buy-and-hold there. The largest-position share stayed mostly in the 40–60% range for the rest of the run, and returns held up. Keep the cap, but make it firmer.
- Lesson 2 (avoid high turnover): The agent often rotated between stocks, but the rotations were small. The gap held steady or widened through them, so the lesson is not clearly supported. Revise it.
- Lesson 3 (don't sell big blocks after a drop): The record doesn't show it hurting or helping clearly, so keep it mild.

LESSON: Hold a core position through ordinary weekly dips and add to the stock that is working. Trim only when a position is far above the others. Most of the gap came from long stretches of "no trades" while the portfolio sat in a balanced, mostly winning mix.

WHY: Holding without trading kept the gap steady or widening while the portfolio rose. Trading was not needed to keep the lead.

LESSON: Cut the weakest stock early and move the proceeds into the stocks that are holding up. Keep doing this while the weak stock keeps falling, but do not let the stronger holding go above about 60–65% of the portfolio.

<lessons>
1. Hold a core position through ordinary weekly dips instead of trading every week. Long stretches without trades kept the lead growing, so only trade when there is a clear reason.
2. Cut the weakest stock early when it keeps falling, and move the proceeds into the stocks that are holding up. Do not let any one holding rise above about 60–65% of the portfolio.
3. Once well ahead of buy-and-hold, avoid repeated small switches between stocks. Frequent small rotations did not add to the lead, and the gap slowly shrank during them.
</lessons>
