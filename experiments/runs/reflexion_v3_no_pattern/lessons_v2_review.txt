Judging the three existing lessons against the record:

- **Lesson 1 (don't chase one-week moves):** Partly helped. The agent kept its core holdings for long stretches, and the gap widened during those periods. It still made many small sell-and-buy switches that were not tied to sustained weakness, and some of these came right before drops.
- **Lesson 2 (cap positions at 60–70%):** Helped. Concentration peaked at about 70%, and the agent trimmed it when it got that high. Its max drawdown (-17.7%) was better than buy-and-hold's (-20.2%).
- **Lesson 3 (protect the lead):** Mostly helped. The gap grew from about +3% to +30% while the agent held a working portfolio. Because it was vague, it didn't stop the agent from rotating.

LESSON: Keep the cap on any single position at about 70%, and trim back toward 55–60% when a position goes above 65%. Do this by selling part of the winner, not by going to cash.

<lessons>
1. Keep any single position at or below about 70% of the portfolio. When one goes above about 65%, trim part of the winner back toward 55–60%, and keep the proceeds invested in the other holdings.
2. Hold a diversified core and avoid same-week switches between holdings. Rotate a position only after it has lagged for several weeks, not because of one weak or strong week.
3. When a holding is falling for several weeks in a row, sell it in steps instead of waiting. Don't rebuy a holding that is still declining.
4. Once ahead of buy-and-hold, prefer making no trades in weeks without a clear sustained signal. Most of the lead was built while the agent held its portfolio.
</lessons>
