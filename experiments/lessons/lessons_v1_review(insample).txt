LESSON: Don't trim into cash or rotate out of a stock because of a short-term move. Rebalancing from one stock into another week after week repeatedly lost ground to simply holding. Make a switch only when you have a clear reason, and keep exposure close to equal-weight otherwise.
EVIDENCE:
Week 69 | Day 370 | SELL STOCK_B 100, BUY STOCK_A 99 | agent value $148,659 | buy & hold $157,573 | gap -5.7%
Week 70 | Day 375 | SELL STOCK_B 50, BUY STOCK_C 52 | agent value $148,911 | buy & hold $159,592 | gap -6.7%
Week 71 | Day 380 | SELL STOCK_B 150, BUY STOCK_A 143, BUY STOCK_A 142 | agent value $154,471 | buy & hold $166,395 | gap -7.2%
Week 48 | Day 265 | SELL STOCK_C 100, BUY STOCK_B 103 | agent value $134,267 | buy & hold $138,691 | gap -3.2%
Week 49 | Day 270 | SELL STOCK_A 120, BUY STOCK_C 147 | agent value $135,876 | buy & hold $140,633 | gap -3.4%
Week 50 | Day 275 | SELL STOCK_A 1, BUY STOCK_B 1 | agent value $139,176 | buy & hold $145,708 | gap -4.5%
WHY: In both runs the agent traded almost every week, and the gap widened each week from -5.7% to -7.2% and from -3.2% to -4.5%. It was moving money between stocks and never caught up with the equal-weight hold. Across the whole record the gap went from about 0% in the first weeks to -7.6% at the end, and the trades never narrowed it for long. Buy-and-hold did better.

LESSON: Don't sell into a sharp one-week drop and sit in cash. In this record, drops were often followed by rebounds, and sell-only weeks or cash left idle during a rebound widened the gap. If you sell, redeploy the proceeds promptly.
EVIDENCE:
Week 99 | Day 520 | SELL STOCK_C 68, SELL STOCK_B 57 | agent value $160,536 | buy & hold $174,841 | gap -8.2%
Week 100 | Day 525 | BUY STOCK_B 100, BUY STOCK_C 120, BUY STOCK_A 50 | agent value $171,024 | buy & hold $189,392 | gap -9.7%
Week 103 | Day 540 | SELL STOCK_C 50, SELL STOCK_B 30 | agent value $157,033 | buy & hold $173,936 | gap -9.7%
Week 104 | Day 545 | BUY STOCK_C 70 | agent value $163,226 | buy & hold $183,341 | gap -11.0%
Week 115 | Day 600 | SELL STOCK_A 213 | agent value $139,287 | buy & hold $152,179 | gap -8.5%
Week 116 | Day 605 | BUY STOCK_B 300, BUY STOCK_C 250, BUY STOCK_A 200 | agent value $139,287 | buy & hold $156,060 | gap -10.7%
WHY: Each time, the agent sold after buy-and-hold had dropped, then the market rebounded. Buy-and-hold gained back more than the agent did: from $174,841 to $189,392, from $173,936 to $183,341, and from $152,179 to $156,060. The gap widened by 1.5, 1.3 and 2.2 points. Week 116 shows the agent's value unchanged at $139,287, so it was fully in cash while the market rose. The cash only gave the agent its protection after the drop. By then the rebound was missed.

<lessons>
1. Don't rotate between stocks or trim positions every week based on short-term moves. Keep holdings near equal-weight and change them only with a clear reason, because constant switching repeatedly lost ground to simply holding.
2. Don't sell into a sharp one-week drop and sit in cash. Drops were often followed by rebounds that the agent missed while in cash. If you sell, redeploy the proceeds promptly rather than leaving them idle.
</lessons>
